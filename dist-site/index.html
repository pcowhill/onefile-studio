How it works

From blank canvas to a file anyone can open

  1. 1

    Create

    Start from a blank canvas or a polished template. Drag in text, pictures, video, charts or 3D models — the defaults already look good.

  2. 2

    Make it interactive

    Pick “when clicked”, “when a key is pressed” or “when the slide opens”, then choose what happens: reveal, animate, score, play, turn the 3D view…

  3. 3

    Export

    One click makes a single, self-contained HTML file — with your media, fonts, interactions and the engine that plays them inside.

  4. 4

    Share

    Email it, attach it to a ticket, put it on a USB stick or your intranet. People double-click and interact — no install, no account.

Why one file?

Everything travels together

  • It contains everything. Content, pictures, video, sound, 3D models, fonts and the playback engine are packed inside.
  • No server, install or account. Double-click it and it opens in any modern browser.
  • Works offline. The file never loads anything from the internet — it runs on a plane, behind a firewall or on an isolated network.
  • Share it like any file. E-mail, chat, a USB stick, a shared drive or a ticket attachment.
  • Re-open it to keep editing. Drop it on OneFile Studio: the whole project is inside the file.

Links you add to websites are ordinary links and need a connection when clicked. Big videos make big files — the export dialog shows the size before you download.

Ideas

What will you make?

  • Presentations

    Meeting decks with reveals and clickable details — that attendees can still explore afterwards.

  • Tutorials

    Screenshots, short videos and numbered callouts people click through at their own pace.

  • Technical explanations

    Architecture diagrams you step through with the keyboard, and 3D models people can turn.

  • Announcements

    Event pages with a live countdown, the programme and a little animation.

  • Interactive learning

    Explain, then quiz: instant feedback, a live score and a satisfying finish.

  • Creative experiments

    Tiny games, birthday greetings, playful “click me” surprises — with springy, draggable things.
